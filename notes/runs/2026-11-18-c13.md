## Declared before any C13 code ran

### Kind of a tracing

A tracing is *elemental* when every footprint is a single site. Every other
tracing is a *pattern* tracing. Elemental tracings are judged by clause E,
pattern tracings by clause P.

### Clause E (elements)

A step t → t+1 of an elemental tracing is carried when either:

- **site:** the footprint is the same site at t and t+1 (every cellular
  automaton: the rule computes each site's next state at that site); or
- **token:** the rule conserves the number of live sites, the footprint is
  live at t and at t+1, and the move from t to t+1 is the move the rule's
  local flow assigns.

The flow is derived from the transition function only: for a one-dimensional
radius-1 rule, the function φ on pairs of adjacent sites with
f(x₋₁, x₀, x₁) = x₀ + φ(x₋₁, x₀) − φ(x₀, x₁) for all eight neighbourhoods and
φ(0, 0) = 0, found by enumerating φ with values in {−1, 0, 1}. A live site p
moves right when φ(a_p, a_{p+1}) = 1, left when φ(a_{p−1}, a_p) = −1, and stays
otherwise. Conservation is checked from the table; the Game of Life does not
conserve, so only the site half applies there.

### Clause P (patterns)

A step t → t+1 of a pattern tracing is carried when closeness holds and the
stage F_t is integrated.

**Closeness:** C10's, unchanged. F_{t+1} is the unique closest continuation of
F_t and F_t the unique closest predecessor of F_{t+1}; closeness is the exact
Jaccard overlap with what a stage alone produces in one step; comparison sets
are single pieces within one cell, plus the empty stage. Ties carry nothing.

**Integration in context.** Pieces of F_t: its 8-connected blobs (rule 184:
runs of consecutive cars). For a set of pieces P, H^{−P} is the history rerun
from t with P's live cells set to 0 at t and everything else as it was. The
dependents of P at step u are the cells where H[u] and H^{−P}[u] differ. Two
groups P and Q *interact* over the window (t, t₁], with t₁ the tracing's last
step, when the contrast H ⊕ H^{−P} ⊕ H^{−Q} ⊕ H^{−P∪Q} is 1 at some cell at
some step of the window. A *split* of F_t is a partition of its pieces into
two groups that do not interact over the window. Computed by: the pairwise
contrast graph on pieces (pairs whose dependents never come within two cells
are skipped, since they cannot co-affect a cell), its connected components,
and the group contrast checked exactly for each bipartition of components.

The variants differ in what a split does:

- **C13a (window ruling as written):** any split ends integration at t. The
  step is not integrated if the two groups' dependents at t₁ are three or more
  cells apart or one group has none; otherwise it is *unresolved* (C10's
  rule).
- **C13b (persisting parts):** a split ends integration only if both groups
  still have dependents at t₁. A group whose effects die out inside the window
  does not split the stage. Otherwise as C13a.
- **C13c (connected history):** the tracing is integrated when its pieces,
  over the whole window, form one connected graph under one-step dependence: a
  piece at t+1 is joined to a piece at t when setting that piece's live cells
  to 0 at t changes some cell of it at t+1. One flag for the whole tracing,
  applied to every step.

C13b and C13c each contain a by-product rule (b: parts that die; c: parts that
descend from the whole). By the 16 Nov ruling they are judged on boards they
have not seen (below).

### Threshold

contrast. The one constant is C10's three-cell distance for "finish apart",
fixed by the rule's reach (two sites three apart share no neighbour). ±20% of
3 rounds to 3, so the sensitivity check leaves every verdict unchanged by
construction; reported as such.

## What this shows

1. **The frozen probe misses are ties.** On the registered tracings every
   variant fails the interaction at steps 16 and 27, and the eater's two
   parts at step 49. At each, a fragment of one to three cells dies at the
   next step. The stage with the fragment and the stage without it produce
   the same next stage, so they are equally close predecessors, and a tie
   carries nothing. C10's run reported that closeness never caused its
   misses; steps 16, 27 and 49 are closeness ties under C10's own code
   (checked by script). C13a also fails integration one step earlier, at the
   split.
2. **So no variant of the closest continuer can carry a tracing that keeps a
   dying fragment until it dies.** Integration is not the obstacle. This is
   the by-product ruling, found by the closeness test: a stage that holds an
   independent fragment ties with the stage without it.
3. **With the fragments removed (fork F2), the eater's sides hold on every
   board.** Registered and three unseen boards: the interaction and the
   eater's persistence carried, the glider refused as continuing into the
   merged piece, under all three variants. The glider interactions hold on
   the registered board and one unseen board; on two unseen boards the
   by-product rule continues the tracing past the interaction's end into one
   of its products, and closeness refuses that step (the product's own
   previous piece is the closer predecessor). That is a fault of the tracing
   construction at the interaction's end, recorded as found.
4. **The car holds by clause E.** The flow derived from rule 184's table is
   φ(1, 0) = 1 and 0 otherwise; the probe's car moves exactly as it says. A
   car splice and the advance convention are refused at the first step that
   leaves the flow. The 127 crossed cells hold as sites. Clause E settles
   both elemental sides the pattern candidates refused.
5. **Integration in context refuses fed bundles.** Two eaters, each eating its
   own glider, traced as one: refused from step 0 by all three variants.
   C10, which runs parts alone, holds the same bundle for 47 steps and
   refuses it only once each merged piece reproduces its own run alone.
6. **The variants differ only where a part has split off for good.** C13c
   (connected history) holds the products of an interaction traced as one
   from contact (N-1b) and refuses the same products traced from the
   interval's end (N-1): its verdict on what follows the interaction depends
   on where the tracing starts. C13a and C13b refuse both. C13b's exemption
   for dying parts changes no verdict, because closeness ties at the death
   step anyway.
7. **Collisions on unseen boards** break at the crash under every variant (3
   of 3).
