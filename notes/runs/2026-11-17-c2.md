# C2: local statistical complexity

Candidate C2 (`notes/CASES.md`). Source: Shalizi, Haslinger, Rouquier, Klinkner
and Moore, "Automatic filters for the detection of coherent structure in
spatiotemporal systems", *PRE* 73, 036104 (2006); causal states from Shalizi
and Crutchfield (2001). Iteration 0 ran a stand-in (an activity filter). This
run builds light-cone causal states from random soups and uses local
statistical complexity as the filter. Rerun: `python3 tools/gol/c2.py`.

## Declared before any C2 code ran

### Light cones

Depth 1 in both directions, the shallowest the method allows.

- **Past cone** of cell x at step t: the 3 × 3 block around x at t − 1. The
  cell's own state at t is a function of that block, so adding it adds
  nothing. Life: 512 pasts. Rule 184: the cell and its two neighbours at
  t − 1, 8 pasts.
- **Future cone:** the 3 × 3 block around x at t + 1 (rule 184: three cells).

Depth 2 (Life: 25 cells two steps back, about 3.4 × 10⁷ possible pasts, futures
of 34 cells) is the cost note below; not run.

### Ensemble for the estimates

Life: 16 random soups, 128 × 128, wrapping, density 0.375, numpy seed 0, run
300 steps; every cell at every step 1–299 is a sample (about 7.8 × 10⁷).
Rule 184: 64 random rings of 240 cells, density 0.5, seed 0, 300 steps. The
transient is kept: the ensemble mixes the chaotic phase with the ash.

### Causal states

Pasts in order of decreasing count. Each past joins the first existing state
whose pooled future distribution it does not differ from, by a χ² test of
homogeneity at α = 0.001 (`scipy.stats.chi2_contingency`, empty future columns
dropped); otherwise it starts a new state. A past seen fewer than 50 times is
its own state. A past never seen has complexity +∞.

**Local statistical complexity** C(x, t) = −log₂ P(the causal state of x's
past). Undefined at step 0.

### The filter

The **domain** is the most probable causal state. K_t: the cells at step t
whose state is not the domain. Fixed by the soup statistics alone; no
threshold is fitted to a case.

### Verdict per step t → t+1 (t ≥ 1)

- **Filter:** at least half the cells of F_{t+1} are in K_{t+1}.
- **Connection:** the cells of K_t and K_{t+1} labelled together as one
  two-layer space-time array, 26-connected (as iteration 0's C2); carried when
  some label touches F_t (within one cell) at t and F_{t+1} (within one cell)
  at t+1.
- **C2:** filter and connection. Filter-only and connection-only flags are
  also reported.

Step 0 → 1 is not judged (K_0 undefined) and is counted. Verdicts go through
the harness as before; collision rows scored there.

### Anchors

The glider must hold and the splice be refused under C2 before any other case
is scored. If they are not, the run stops there and that is the result.

### Rule 9

Board shifted 7 rows and 13 columns; 7 empty frames before the history.

### Cases

The C11, C12 and C3 set.

## Predictions

Frozen, the orchestrator's, labels hyphenated.

- D-1: picks out the glider as a particle.
- D-2: the verdict on splices comes from reach, not from the filter.

How each is judged, fixed now:

1. In the glider case, at every step t ≥ 1, every live glider cell is in
   K_t and every cell of K_t lies within two cells of the glider.
2. Splice and alternating splice: the filter alone passes every judged step;
   C2 refuses each; every refused step fails the connection.

## Cost of depth 2 (feasibility note, written before the run)

Depth 2 needs, per past, enough samples to estimate a distribution over up to
2³⁴ futures. With about 10⁵–10⁶ distinct pasts seen in soups, a χ² test per
past against each state, and a few hundred samples per past, the estimate
needs 10⁸ or more samples and a sparse joint table. Pure numpy can collect
that in tens of minutes; the clustering and the memory for sparse
distributions are the bottleneck. Out of scope for minutes.

## What this shows

1. **At depth 1 the causal-state filter is an activity filter.** All 512
   pasts are distinct causal states; the domain is the empty past. The
   filter keeps everything near something alive, as iteration 0's stand-in
   did. It shares the stand-in's misses (indirect splice, debris
   collision, union) and adds one: the annihilating collision.
2. **The splices fall to connection, a reach test.** The filter passes them
   at every step. This is D-2, and R1's necessary condition doing the work.
3. **The probes pass where reach suffices.** The car, the jam, the eater and
   both interactions hold because every followed tracing stays joined
   through live cells. The two misses are the bundle (A and B as one) and the
   glider into the merged piece: joined in space-time, so held.
4. **Grading exists and goes unused.** Glider cells score about 1.7 bits
   above still-life cells. A threshold on that grading would be fitted, so it
   was not tried.

## Feasibility of the proper version

Depth 2 or more is where Shalizi et al. find particles against domains; at
depth 1 Life's only domain is empty space. The cost note above stands: 10⁸ or
more samples, a sparse joint table over up to 2³⁴ futures per past, and
clustering of 10⁵–10⁶ pasts. A test that tolerates the sample size (an effect
size floor, or CSSR's per-state tests at a fixed sample budget) is a second
choice the method leaves open. Not run.
