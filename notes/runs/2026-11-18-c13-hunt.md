### Diagnosis (scripts in the session, rerunnable from `hunt13.py`)

- **H-1 is the registered indirect splice.** The search's first board that
  ends as one glider is the boat reaction of the indirect splice row; its
  target is uncarried (owner). The F2 refusal matches that target.
- **H-8, the lightweight spaceship, is refused at every even step.** In those
  phases one live cell is a separate piece that dies at the next step without
  changing any other cell (checked by rerun with the cell set to 0). The stage
  with it and the stage without it produce the same next stage: a closeness
  tie. Integration also splits it off. This is the registered fragment
  failure in a pattern the catalogue counts as one spaceship.
- **The registered fragments are the same kind of cell.** At steps 16 and 27
  of the interaction and step 49 of the eater, the fragment that causes the
  tie was produced by the previous stage and changes nothing afterwards
  (*idle*). At steps 15 and 48 the fragments still change one cell before
  dying.
- **H-4, the pulsar, fails at its last step.** In the phase at step 119 its
  pieces do not affect each other within one step and finish three cells
  apart, so C10's "finish apart" rule calls it a split. Over any longer window
  they interact. The run's end cuts a lull short; the 16 Nov window ruling
  says a run that stops first leaves the end unresolved, and C10's three-cell
  rule does not detect that.
- **H-11 is a code fault.** Pieces and isolated evolution are computed
  without wrap, so a glider crossing the board's edge is cut in two and its
  isolated evolution is wrong. Rule 9 holds for the shifts the runs used;
  it fails for a shift that carries a tracing across the edge. Fix: compute
  isolated evolution and pieces on the wrapping board.
- **H-14: holes.** Rule 184 conserves holes as well as cars, and complement
  with reflection maps the rule to itself and cars to holes. Clause E carries
  cars and refuses holes, so it treats 1 as the token by convention. Owner
  question.
