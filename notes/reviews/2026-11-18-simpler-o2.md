# Outside review, 18 Nov: is O2 posed in a needlessly complicated way?

Reviewer: a separate session, with no part in C1 to C16. IDs with a hyphen
(S-1, T-1, P-1) are this review's own. Rerun: `python3 tools/gol/s1.py
conflicts` and `python3 tools/gol/s1.py score`.

## Answer

Partly. The framing makes the problem harder than it is in one way, and the
targets make it impossible as posed in two places.

The self-inflicted part: O2 is tested as a membership test on hand-built
tracings, judged step by step, against targets set on what the builders
happened to include. C15 builds its history graph from the tracing
(`c15.facts(H, tr, ...)`), so its verdicts move with the tracing's start
(H-23, H-25, N-2) and its end (H-24). Two of its four patches (idle exhaust,
lasting effects) exist to accept fragments that `cases.follow` swept into the
interaction tracings. A criterion computed once from the history removes that
whole class: S-1 below gets H-21 to H-24 right and gives the same verdict from
every start.

The real part: in the Game of Life, interaction is counterfactual (the
bi-block's halves do not interact, the pulsar's pieces do, at the same
distance), and every merger needs a declared continuer. No reframing removes
either. The owner's merger targets do not follow from one size or overlap
convention (T-2), and the separation ruling depends on the board (T-1). Twelve
clauses is what fitting both at once costs.

## 1. Which parts of the setup make it hard

- **Continuity as an equivalence (D1).** It forbids branching, so every merge
  and split needs a continuer. Every candidate since C10 carries a closest
  continuer, and C13's misses were its ties. An event graph keeps D1 for
  what it holds (a track's occurrences are one class) and names the
  convention once, at the events. This removes O4 as a blocker for O2. The
  convention stays.
- **Verdicts per tracing, step by step.** The main source of difficulty,
  shown above. The register judges tracings built by four procedures with
  three notions of "together" (T-3). Each notion becomes a target the
  criterion must fit at boundary steps.
- **Carrying read off the rule.** Necessary and cheap. Reach alone is too
  coarse. S-1's reach links join a whole rule-184 ring (below). Reach
  cannot tell the bi-block from the pulsar. Knockout dependence can.
- **The four ordered checks.** Checks 1 to 3 are a few lines of partition
  arithmetic and never caused a miss. Keep them.
- **Per-step thresholds.** The anchors score 1 and 0, so every threshold
  between them fits the anchors; C10s, C11, C12 and C16 each made a free
  choice there. C13 to C15 dropped thresholds. Already removed.
- **One criterion for every kind.** Already abandoned in practice. C13 to C15
  switch on kind: one clause for elements, the rest for patterns. Every
  pattern candidate refuses the cell and the car; the element clause carries
  127 of 127 crossed cells, every rule-184 car, and the hole once the rule's
  complement-and-reflection symmetry is applied. The evidence supports a
  declared family of two. Nothing in the Game of Life bears on a reflexive
  kind.

## 2. Targets in tension

- **T-1. Separation depends on the board.** The harness runs on a 128 torus.
  `s1.py conflicts`: the Gosper gun's gliders come round and meet the gun at
  step 503. Ruling 2 (unresolved unless provably unable to interact again)
  and the 16 Nov window ruling (integration ends when the parts evolve apart
  for the rest of the observed run) then make the gun and its gliders
  unresolved for a run shorter than 503 steps and one whole for a longer run.
  The principle says they are a bundle. No criterion satisfies all three on
  this board. The registered pair's products are the opposite case: the two
  mirror halves at step 96, run alone, reproduce the history to step 600, and
  the board is period 2 from step 191, so separation is proved and ruling 2
  says refused. C15 says unresolved (N-1, N-1b, H-23 from 96).
- **T-2. "The larger continues" against "the glider ends at the block".** A
  glider has 5 cells, a block 4. At the first contact step the merged group is
  both pieces unchanged, so one-step overlap gives the glider 5/9 and the
  block 4/9. Any size or overlap convention continues the glider into the
  debris; the collision target ends it there. The eater (7 cells) and the jam
  ruling agree with size; the block collision does not. The statement that
  covers eater, jam and collision is form survival: the predecessor whose own
  form the product returns to continues (the literature survey's L-12). That
  needs a window, which is one more free choice.
- **T-3. The register's tracings use three notions of "together".**
  `probes.follow_piece` follows 8-connected blobs (the eater persists);
  `cases.follow` takes cells within one cell of the last stage (the
  interactions); interval starts and ends use distance two and run-apart
  equality (`probes.settled_from`). A criterion with any single notion of
  interacting misses the others at the boundary steps. Eleven of S-1's
  twelve probe and unseen misses are such boundaries.
- **T-4. D8's proposed closure against the eater.** D8 still lists closure
  (each stage depends mainly on the previous stage of the same continuity) as
  proposed. At contact the eater's next stage depends on the glider; C9
  refused the eater for that reason. The eater target holds only through a
  continuer convention, which closure does not supply.
- **T-5. Ruling 2 quantifies over all future time.** On the plane, whether two
  patterns ever interact again is undecidable in general. The register's own
  16 Nov queue note preferred finite stretches. On a finite torus it is
  decidable by running to a cycle, which brings back T-1.

A target set no criterion satisfies: {the gun and its gliders are a bundle;
ruling 2; the window ruling} on any finite wrapping board where the gliders
return within the run. Change the board to the plane, or bound the horizon.

## 3. Simpler formulations

**P-1 (proposed, tested as S-1).** Split D8 into two kinds. Elements: a token
of a conserved quantity moved by the rule's flow, sites being the trivial
case. Patterns: a track in the event graph of the actual history, built once
from the history and the rule: pieces, interaction groups, links, and one
declared crossing rule at merges and splits. Restate the register's targets
as questions to the graph (who continues through each event; which pieces are
one group at a step) and retire the hand-built tracings, or rebuild them from
the graph's one notion of interacting.

**P-2 (rejected): local transfer entropy (Lizier et al.).** C11 and C12 are
already information-flow measures on light cones. Both hold the union of two
distant gliders: transfer adds over parts out of reach, so it cannot refuse a
bundle. Both need an ensemble, and C11's glider scored 0.45 against θ = 0.5
under uniform resampling. Storage, transfer and modification would label
events without deciding who continues. At most, it could serve as the score
inside a crossing rule.

### Definition

S-1 builds a graph from the whole history and the rule, once, and never from
the tracing. A tracing is then checked against it.

- **Kind.** A tracing that is one site at every step is elemental; any other
  is a pattern tracing.
- **E.** An elemental step is carried when the site stays, or, in a rule that
  conserves a state, moves as the rule's flow moves a token of that state. In
  rule 184 this covers cars and, through the rule's complement-and-reflection
  symmetry, holes.
- **P-1 Pieces.** At each step the live cells join into pieces: two cells join
  when each is in the other's neighbourhood (Life: 8-connected; rule 184:
  adjacent).
- **P-2 Idle.** A piece is idle when removing it leaves the rule's next state
  unchanged.
- **P-3 Links.** A piece that is not idle links to each next-step piece with a
  cell in its neighbourhood.
- **P-4 Groups.** Two pieces at one step are in one group when they interact
  (removing both changes the next state otherwise than removing each), or
  when they have a common ancestor and a common descendant in the run (a
  lull). Groups are the classes this generates.
- **P-5 Tracks.** Groups link when their pieces do. A group continues as a
  linked next-step group when each is the other's unique best match. The
  score is the Jaccard overlap of the later group with the earlier group run
  alone for one step. An exact tie continues nothing. A track is a maximal
  chain of continuations.
- **P-6 Dead ends.** A dead end is a group whose descendants all die within
  the run and never share a group with a piece outside its descent. A dead
  end that is no group's continuation belongs to the track it descends from.
  One with no parent belongs to every track within reach (two cells) at its
  step.
- **P-7 Verdict.** A pattern step t to t+1 is carried when the slice at t is a
  group of a track, the slice at t+1 is its continuation, and each slice
  differs from its group only by dead ends belonging to that track.

E and P-1 to P-5 are the formulation. P-6 and P-7 adapt it to the register's
hand-built tracings. There is no threshold: the anchors fix nothing because
there is nothing to fix. The horizon is the observed run. S-1 never returns
*unresolved*.

Free choices: piece adjacency (P-1), the interaction test (pairwise, one step;
P-4), the lull horizon (the run; P-4), the score (one-step Jaccard; P-5), the
tie rule (P-5), dead ends by descent (P-6), parentless dead ends by reach (P-6),
the elemental clause (E). Eight, against C15's twelve clauses (audit, §6).

## Comparison with F-1 and F-2 (literature survey, acd5a63)

S-1 is F-1 with three differences, and the scores locate each.

- Links: F-1 links by knockout, S-1 by reach. S-1's two rule-184 failures are
  reach failures. F-1 is right here.
- Support: F-1 uses Pivato's defects, so the car-hole map sends patterns to
  patterns. S-1 gets the hole only through the element clause. Rule 184's
  pattern side was not tested for symmetry by either.
- Interaction wholes: F-1 ends one at a split into pieces that provably never
  link again; S-1 at the last common descendant in the run. On a torus the
  two coincide only for a run long enough to reach a cycle (T-1).
- Shared: the crossing rule is C10's closest continuer in both. S-1's native
  table is direct evidence against F-1's prediction that "endings into a
  product are refused": with C10's closeness, the glider continues into the
  block's debris on 4 of 5 boards. F-1 needs the form-survival test (its own
  L-12) in place of one-step closeness. F-1 also says nothing about the
  history's first and last steps, where S-1 failed.

F-2 (actual causation chained over steps) addresses T-2 directly: the debris's
actual cause is the glider-and-block pair, so the glider ends at contact
without a size convention. It keeps the per-tracing framing, with its
builder-boundary exposure, and adds two free choices (the k-step window, the
uniform marginalization) and an exponential purview search. Its strength is
exactly S-1's weakness; S-1's strength (one graph per history) is its
weakness. A combination is natural: F-1's graph, with F-2's actual-cause test
as the crossing rule at merge vertices only, where purviews are the merging
pieces and the cost is small.

## Recommendation

Split the problem.

1. Elements: adopt the flow-token clause with the symmetry (sites, cars,
   holes). It is one sentence and has not failed.
2. Patterns: pose O2 as the event graph of the history plus one crossing
   rule. The graph is mechanical once knockout links and a history-start rule
   are fixed. The crossing rule is a convention, and the owner sets it after
   ruling on T-2 (form survival or size).
3. Targets: restate them as questions to the graph, and rebuild or retire the
   hand-built tracings, which encode three notions of "together" (T-3).
   Fix the board (plane, or a horizon) before ruling 2 can be scored (T-1).
4. Keep C15 as a reference result. Its frozen match is partly a fit to the
   builders' conventions, and S-1 shows that the hunter cases it fails come
   from computing on the tracing.

S-1 is not ready to replace C15: 6 of 10 registered probes. Its next version
is F-1 with knockout links, with the crossing rule left open for the owner.
