# Where the test stops

The test refuses a pair for type, coincidence or nesting. A pair that
cross-cuts it cannot yet refuse (O2). The checks run in
order (§6 of [`specs/ARTICULATION.md`](../specs/ARTICULATION.md)), and each
case below stops at a different one. Terms are in the
[README](../README.md#two-ways-of-counting), credits in
[PRIOR-ART.md](../PRIOR-ART.md).

## Refused, untyped: a cone

A cone's sections include a triangle and a circle. But a cone does not
change, so there are no occurrences in a changing for any criterion of
sameness to trace (P2). `triangle < cone > circle` is not false. It is not a
claim.

## Refused, coincident: Locke's mass of matter

Take some matter whose atoms join into masses and later part. For
Locke, atoms joined into a mass are the same mass exactly while the same
atoms stay united; add or remove one atom and it is another mass (*Essay*
II.xxvii.3). So "same mass" and "same united atoms" never come apart over
that matter's changing. They are one criterion under two names (D5).

## Refused, nested: temperature and microstate

Take a gas whose molecules move and collide. Its microstate is the exact
position and velocity of every molecule. For an ideal gas, temperature
measures the molecules' mean kinetic energy, which the microstate fixes. So
sameness of microstate guarantees sameness of temperature, and not
conversely: the same temperature is reached by countless microstates. One
criterion refines the other, so they do not cross-cut (D6).

Nesting ranks neither side. Temperature is not a fiction because microstate
is finer (R3).

## Not refused, uncarried: green and grue

Goodman (1955) defined *grue*: green if examined before a fixed date, blue
otherwise. Its partner *bleen* is blue if examined before the date, green
otherwise.

Take as the whole a run of examinations of emeralds and sapphires, one
examination per gem, spanning the date. The examinations are the
occurrences. Two examinations are the same by colour when the gems examined
share a colour, and the same by grue when both gems are grue or both bleen.
Each is a sameness relation on the occurrences, so each is a candidate
criterion (D1).

- *Same colour, different grue-class.* An emerald examined before the date
  (grue) and an emerald examined after it (bleen).
- *Same grue-class, different colour.* An emerald examined before the date
  and a sapphire examined after it: both grue, one green and one blue.

The pair cross-cuts in both directions, just as cell and glider do.
Intuition says grue is laid across the examinations rather than sustained by
their changing. The test cannot yet say so, because what separates a carried
criterion from one laid across is undefined (O2). The verdict is
**uncarried**: undecided, not refused. Until O2 is answered, the test can
refuse a pair for type, coincidence or nesting, but not for arbitrariness.
