# Limits

Most pairs put to the test do not pass. A theory that said yes to everything
would say nothing, so these cases matter as much as the glider. The checks run
in order (§6 of `specs/ARTICULATION.md`); each failure below stops at a
different one.

## Untyped: a cone

A cone's cross-sections include triangles and circles. But a cone does not
change. There are no occurrences in a changing, so no criterion of sameness has
anything to trace.

    triangle < cone > circle

is not a false claim. It is not a claim. Anything static stops here, however
elegantly its aspects correspond (P2).

## Coincident: charge and field

In electrostatics, the charge distribution fixes the field and the field fixes
the charge. So two occurrences agree on one exactly when they agree on the
other. The two criteria never come apart: one continuity under two names (D5).

## Nested: temperature and microstate

Two occurrences can share a temperature while differing in microstate. Two
occurrences with the same microstate cannot differ in temperature. One
criterion refines the other, so they do not cross-cut (D6).

Nesting privileges neither side (R3). Temperature is not a fiction because
microstate is finer.

## Open: colour and shape

Take cars in a car park. Same colour, different shape; same shape, different
colour. The criteria cross-cut in both directions, just as cell and glider do.

Yet nothing in the car park seems to *carry* them: they look laid across it, a
sorting rather than an articulation. The kernel cannot yet say so. What
separates a carried criterion from an imposed one is the theory's central open
problem (O2). Until it is answered, the test can refuse a pair for type, for
coincidence and for nesting, but not for arbitrariness.
