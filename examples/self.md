# The self

The case of the person. Terms are in [the kernel](../specs/ARTICULATION.md),
credits in [PRIOR-ART.md](../PRIOR-ART.md), and IDs point to [`specs/ARTICULATION.md`](../specs/ARTICULATION.md).

## The case

We say "I think", "I feel", "I want". Look for the one who thinks among the
body, the thoughts, the sensations and the perceptions, and none of them is
it. Hume (*Treatise* I.iv.6) found only a bundle of perceptions. The Buddhist
doctrine of *anattā* finds no self among the aggregates. Lichtenberg proposed
saying "it thinks", as one says "it lightens".

## Two criteria

- **Matter.** The same atoms, wherever they go.
- **Self.** The same person, through any change of matter, thought and
  sensation.

Both directions are witnessed:

- *Same self, different matter.* A person over ten years, while metabolism
  replaces most of the body's atoms.
- *Same matter, different selves.* An atom that was part of one person and is
  later part of another, through food and decay.

The candidate is `matter < life > self`, in the same shape as Locke's oak:
one life through changes of matter.

**Verdict.** Life changes, the criteria differ, and they cross-cut (D7).
Whether life's changing carries the self is undecided (D8, O2), so the verdict
is **uncarried**, pending.

## A second pair

`body < life > self` is the debate about personal identity. Animalism (Olson
1997) holds that we are animals: self and body coincide, or the self is nested
in the body. The psychological view (Locke; Parfit 1984) lets them come apart.
In ICW terms the two sides disagree on check 2 or 3. A cross-cut needs a
witness in each direction. *Same body, different selves*: the split-brain cases
(Gazzaniga), on the contested reading that one body then hosts two selves.
*Same self, different bodies*: only thought experiments, Parfit's
teletransporter and fission (O4).

## Two carriers

The case proposes that two relations sustain the self:

1. **Integration.** The streams of sensation, thought and movement bind into
   one and do not split into parts that each go on alone.
2. **Self-dependence.** Each moment takes more from the same system's previous
   moment (memory, body, standing goals) than from its surroundings. This is
   C11's inflow, applied to a mind.

## Probes

Each is a published finding, read in this case's terms. The panel judges
them; no script can.

- **Rubber hand illusion** (Botvinick and Cohen 1998). Synchronous stroking
  brings a rubber hand into what feels like one's own; asynchronous stroking
  does not. The boundary of the self follows integration, not anatomy.
- **Split brain.** After the cerebral commissures are cut, integration fails,
  and in tests the person at times acts as two. A branching case (O4).
- **Dense amnesia.** Clive Wearing, after encephalitis in 1985, keeps new
  memories for seconds and remains himself from moment to moment. Memory is
  cut and integration holds; the self persists, thinner.
- **Dreamless sleep and anaesthesia.** The pattern stops and returns the same.
  A test of whether carrying can bridge a gap.

**Prediction.** Break integration and the self splits or shrinks. Break memory
and keep integration, and the self persists, thinner.

## What the case adds

It is the first case with two carriers, each of which can fail while the
other holds. If both are needed, carrying here is a family of relations (rule
10), one per carrier. The self is also the case where the pattern models
itself, as money's holders model money (Metzinger 2003).

**"There is no self."** True of the bundle: no element among the perceptions
is the self (D3). The self is a pattern of the bundle, and whether life
carries it is the open question.
