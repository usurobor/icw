# Money

## The case

A banknote passes from hand to hand. Each holder takes it because they can
pay with it in turn: buy goods, settle a debt, pay a tax. Over the years every
note in circulation wears out and is replaced, and most payments now move no
paper at all. The currency continues through all of this.

The reverse also happens. In 1919 the successor states of Austria-Hungary
stamped the krone notes circulating on their territory: the Kingdom of Serbs,
Croats and Slovenes in January, Czechoslovakia in February and March, German
Austria in March. One printed note became Yugoslav, Czechoslovak or Austrian
money according to its stamp. The stamp decided which currency the note
settled debts in; the paper stayed the same.

## Two criteria

- **Note.** The same piece of printed paper, wherever it goes.
- **Currency.** The same means of settlement, through any change of notes
  and ledger entries.

Both directions are witnessed:

- *Same currency, different notes.* The currency before and after its notes
  are replaced. A debt paid in cash and a debt paid by transfer.
- *Same note, different currencies.* An Austro-Hungarian krone note in 1918,
  and the same note in 1919, stamped, as Czechoslovak money.

The candidate is `note < economy > currency`: the economy's changing, every
payment, issue and withdrawal, contains every occurrence of both.

**Verdict.** The economy changes, the criteria differ, and they cross-cut
(D7). Whether its changing carries both is undecided (D8, O2), so the verdict
is **uncarried**, pending. The owner's view (17 Nov): the currency is carried,
and the observable practice of settlement is what carries it.

## Two sentences

**"Money is just paper."** True only of its footprint: at an instant, the
currency's footprint is some notes and ledger entries (D3). If the pair were
articulated, the persisting currency would be made of no particular notes
(R4, proposed).

**"Money exists only because people believe in it"** (Harari 2014). This
places the currency in a state of each holder. The currency's criterion is
settlement, and whether a note settles a debt is checked in the practice,
whatever its holder thinks. Both directions are witnessed. A holder who
thinks money a fraud still takes it, because they can pay with it. A group
can agree that a board game's notes are worth something, and still cannot pay
rent with them.

What a holder has is an expectation. They have seen notes settle debts many
times and expect them to keep doing so, as they expect the sun to rise. The
expectation is learned from the pattern.

## What the case adds

The holders form a third continuity in the same economy. They pass notes
along, form expectations by watching the currency work, and take notes
because of those expectations. The pattern is sustained partly through its
elements' record of its own past. Cells in the Game of Life keep no record of
the glider, so this case breaks an assumption of iteration 0: its elements
are reflexive. When enough holders expect a currency to fail, they stop taking
it, and it fails. A bank run has this shape.

**For O2.** Closure asks whether each stage depends mainly on the previous
stage of the same continuity. Here each settlement depends on earlier
settlements, through holders' expectations. Whether dependence that runs
through the elements' record of the pattern counts toward closure is open.
Lewis's (1969) conventions have the same structure: a regularity that
persists because each expects the others to conform. Menger (1892) traces
money to exchange, as traders converge on the goods that sell most easily.
