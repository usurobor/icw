# Perception

*"There is no red flower. There are only fields, light and neural signals."*

ICW refutes half of this sentence and leaves the other half open. IDs refer to
`specs/ARTICULATION.md`.

## What it refutes: the step to non-existence

The argument goes: physics gives a complete account of the flower in other
terms, so the flower isn't really there. The step does not follow. A second
articulation of a history removes nothing from the first. Derivability does not
settle identity (R2), and when one continuity refines another, neither is
privileged (R3). Discovering another way to count does not make the things
already counted disappear.

## What it does not establish: that the flower is real

ICW has no predicate *real*. Its strongest condition is *carried* (D8), and
carrying is not yet defined (O2). So the defensible claim is conditional: *if*
the flower's continuity is carried, nothing in a field description undercuts
it. The kernel's nomenclature rules out reading "carried" as "real".

## Typing the case

The cross-cut that clearly holds is not flower and field but flower and
molecules:

    molecule < plant > flower

A molecule moves from the stem into the flower: same molecule, a change in
whether it belongs to the flower. The flower persists while its molecules turn
over: same flower, different molecules. Cross-cutting holds. Carrying is open.

"Field" in the sceptic's sentence mostly means the light reaching the eye,
which belongs to a different whole. Paired wrongly, the case yields a mistyped
verdict, not a refutation.

## Hoffman's interface theory

Donald Hoffman argues that perception is tuned to fitness rather than truth,
so perceived objects are like desktop icons. Two claims are worth separating:

- **A.** Perceptual categories do not resemble the structure of other
  articulations. ICW *expects* this. Cross-cutting articulations, by
  definition, do not preserve each other's identities.
- **B.** Nothing carried by the world corresponds to a perceived object. That
  is a claim about carrying, so it is open (O2), and the answer depends on
  which whole is named. A rainbow fails if the whole is the droplets alone. It
  may not fail if the whole is sun, droplets and observer together.

ICW grants A and denies that A implies B.

## Redness

Sameness of colour is the kernel's stock example of a criterion not shown to
be carried. Red is not a continuity of the flower. Whether it is carried by the
larger whole of flower, light and observer is the same open question as the
rainbow.

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A field description makes the flower unreal | Invalid inference (R2, R3) |
| `molecule < plant > flower` | Cross-cuts; carrying open (O2) |
| Perceived objects are interface fictions | A granted; B open (O2) |
| Red belongs to the flower | Not a continuity of the flower |
