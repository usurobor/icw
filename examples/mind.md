# Mind and brain

The case where "emerges from" carries the most weight. Terms are in
[the kernel](../specs/ARTICULATION.md), credits in
[PRIOR-ART.md](../PRIOR-ART.md), and IDs point to
[`specs/ARTICULATION.md`](../specs/ARTICULATION.md).

## The computer first

A running computer can be followed by two criteria.

- **Circuit.** The same circuit, through any change in the computation it
  takes part in.
- **Variable.** The same variable, through any change in the circuits that
  hold it.

A memory cell holds one variable, is freed, then holds another. A variable
moved to another address stays, for the program, one variable.
`circuit < the running computer > variable` cross-cuts (D7): **uncarried**,
pending (O2).

## Four sentences

**"A variable needs a circuit to hold it."** That is dependence, and it ranks
neither criterion (R3). A circuit needs matter to hold it too, and the matter
outlasts the circuit. Ranking by holder runs down to physics.

**"No transistor stores a variable."** No transistor carries the machine's
electrical activity either. Both criteria are whole-level, as in the brain:
no neuron feels pain, and no neuron carries the brain's activity.

**"Neural activity produces experience."** Cause and effect are two events.
Where a pair is articulated, a causal claim across it is about one change
(R5, proposed). It can be true. It names no second process.

**"Experience emerges."** As a second event, experience either does nothing
the neural activity has not done or gives every action two causes (Kim 1998).
As the same process, "emerges" names a whole-level criterion, already granted.

## The brain

Experiential sameness, what makes this the same pain a moment later, has no
stated criterion, so brain and experience cannot reach check 2. A neural pair
can reach check 3: `neuron < the brain's activity > attractor`, where an
attractor is a pattern the population returns to. A head-direction cell fires
at one heading and is quiet at the others. In mouse parietal cortex, neurons
followed for weeks changed what they encoded while the task stayed readable
(Driscoll et al. 2017; Rule et al. 2020). Cross-cut, **uncarried**, pending.

That suggests an experiential criterion: same experience, same trajectory on
the same attractor. If they coincide (D5), that is identity theory moved from
neurons to dynamics. Different animals converge on the same kind of attractor;
relating wholes is O5.

The verdicts sort the rivals. Identity theory (Place 1956, Smart 1959) needs
coincidence. Emergence with a direction needs nesting, which ranks neither
(R3). Co-articulation needs cross-cut and carrying.

## Open

Why there is experience at all (Chalmers 1995) becomes: why does this whole
carry an experiential criterion? That is O2 for this case. Searle (1990)
holds that physics does not fix which program runs; Chalmers (1996) and
Piccinini (2015) answer that causal structure does. If they are right, the
computer case ends at **nested**.
