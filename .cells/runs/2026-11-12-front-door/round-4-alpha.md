## Quine check (web)

I re-read Quine 1950 in the *From a Logical Point of View* text (Charles
University copy, ch. IV). He says a river is "a process through time, and the
river stages are its momentary parts". He reads "this river" as "the riverish
summation of momentary objects which contains this momentary object". He
says "River stages are water stages, but rivers are not waters". He
distinguishes "concrete parts in a concrete whole" from "concrete instances
in an abstract universal".

So Quine already treats composition as fixed by the grouping: the stages
that make up the river are fixed by river kinship. I could not verify that
the candidate narrowing, a composition-vs-identity distinction for "consists
of", is absent from him. His parts/identity remarks suggest it may not be. I
therefore took option (b), as instructed.
